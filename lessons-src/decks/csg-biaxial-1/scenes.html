Biaxial Gemstones

Orthorhombic, monoclinic and triclinic gems have two optic axes. On the refractometer both shadow edges can move, and many are trichroic.

Reminder · uniaxial vs biaxial

Two optic axes, three RIs

1

Uniaxial

One shadow edge stays fixed (ω), the other moves (ε). Tetragonal, hexagonal and trigonal.

2

Biaxial

Three principal RIs (α, β, γ): both shadow edges can move as you rotate. Orthorhombic, monoclinic and triclinic.

3

Trichroism

Biaxial coloured stones can show three colours in different directions, two at a time in the dichroscope.

Alexandrite · “emerald by day, ruby by night”
Bluish green

Daylight is rich in blue-green light, so the stone transmits and looks green.

Chromium absorbs yellow light and leaves two windows, red and green. The light source decides which one wins: the colour-change effect.

Natural and synthetic alexandrite

Natural

Russia (Urals), Sri Lanka, Brazil, Tanzania, India. Pleochroism green, orange and purplish red.

Synthetic (flux)

Platinum platelets and flux feathers prove synthesis. Colour-change synthetic corundum and spinel are cheaper imitations.
