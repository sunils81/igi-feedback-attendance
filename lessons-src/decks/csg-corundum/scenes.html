Varieties of corundum

Ruby

All shades of red: pinkish, orangy and purplish red, and star ruby. From Latin rubrum, red. The “king of precious stones”, birthstone for July; Manek in India.

Sapphire

“Sapphire” alone means blue. Every other colour takes a prefix: yellow, pink, green, violet, orange, colourless, padparadscha, colour-change, star. Birthstone for September; Neelam (blue) and Pukhraj (yellow).

Colour

Pure corundum is colourless. A trace of chromium, iron, titanium or vanadium paints it.

What the market wants

Ruby

Purplish red to red, medium-light to medium-dark tone, strong to vivid saturation. The top: “pigeon’s blood”, a fiery red of medium tone and vivid saturation, seen in the finest Burmese rubies.

Blue sapphire

Medium-light to dark, moderate to vivid violetish blue: “royal blue”. Rarest: “cornflower blue”, a velvety, strongly saturated violetish blue of the finest Kashmir sapphires.

Reading the origin

Each deposit leaves a signature of inclusions. Ruby comes from Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, Sri Lanka, Mozambique, Madagascar, Tanzania and more; sapphire also from Kashmir, Australia, Montana and Africa.

    Section 04 · treatments · to deepen, change or remove colour, improve clarity, create phenomena

    Heat

    Most rubies and sapphires are heated to improve colour. Permanent and the most common.

    Flux healing

    Heated with borax or another flux, which heals fractures.

    Beryllium diffusion

    Beryllium driven in at high temperature changes colour (e.g. orange, padparadscha-like).

    Glass filling

    Lead glass fills fractures to improve clarity. Must be disclosed; fragile.

    Care & cleaning

    Warm soapy water is always safe. Ultrasonic is fine for untreated and heated stones. Glass-filled stones: a damp cloth only.

    Synthetics: same chemistry and properties, different inclusions · flame fusion (Verneuil), flux, hydrothermal

    Ruby simulants

    Red spinel, rubellite tourmaline, dyed quartz, almandine, rhodolite, pyrope garnet, red glass.

    Yellow sapphire simulants

    Topaz, citrine, yellow tourmaline.

    Blue sapphire simulants

    Blue topaz, tanzanite, iolite, kyanite, aquamarine, cobalt-blue (synthetic) spinel, blue tourmaline.

    RI 1.76–1.77 and SG 4.00 rule most of them out; inclusions separate synthetics.
