What the dichroscope shows

Pleochroism is a difference in colour, or depth of colour, seen in different directions through a coloured stone.

It comes from selective absorption of light in doubly refractive stones: the two rays are absorbed differently.

Group I · SR

No effect: one colour.

Group II · uniaxial

Two colours: dichroic.

Group III · biaxial

Three colours: trichroic (two at a time).

Limitations and good practice

Cannot test

  • Opaque stones
  • Colourless stones

Remember

  • No pleochroism does not prove SR: look along the optic axis and a DR stone shows one colour too
  • Turn the stone and look in several directions
  • Use bright white light, held close
What we learned

Pleochroism

Colour changes with direction, from selective absorption in DR stones.

Results

SR: none · uniaxial: dichroic · biaxial: trichroic. Strength weak, moderate or strong.

Limits

Not for opaque or colourless stones; look in several directions.
