Emerald

The green of chromium and vanadium in beryl. Prized for thousands of years, nearly always included, and almost always oiled.

A stone of royalty · from Latin smaragdus

Egypt

Some of the earliest emerald mines were in Egypt, later called “Cleopatra’s mines” for her love of the stone.

The New World

In the 16th century Spanish conquerors found Colombian emeralds and traded them to European and Asian royalty, including the Mughals.

Beryl family

Beryllium aluminium silicate coloured by traces of chromium (and vanadium). The finest come from Colombia.

Section 01 · the greens

Most desired

Grass green or “Muzo green”, a slightly bluish green of medium tone and vivid saturation.

In the market

Yellowish green to bluish green, medium-light to medium tone, strong saturation. Most are heavily included: the inclusions are called the jardin, the garden.

Green beryl vs emerald

Light green beryl coloured by iron is not emerald. Emerald needs chromium or vanadium and enough saturation.

    Section 04 · treatments

    Oiling: before (left) and after (right). Oil fills surface-reaching fractures so they almost disappear.

    Oiling

    Very common: most emeralds in the market are oiled. Coloured oil amounts to dyeing.

    Resin filling

    Hardened resins fill fractures more permanently.

    Dyed colourless beryl

    Green dye in fractures of pale beryl imitates emerald.

    Care: warm soapy water and a soft brush. No ultrasonic or steam: they sweat the oil out.
    Synthetics: same properties, cheaper, betrayed by inclusions · look-alikes
    What we learned

    Emerald

    Beryl with chromium/vanadium. Muzo green; jardin of inclusions.

    Properties

    Hexagonal, H 7.5–8 but brittle, SG 2.71, RI 1.577–1.583, biref 0.007.

    Origin and treatment

    Three-phase (Colombia), biotite (Zambia), actinolite (Russia), tremolite (Zimbabwe). Oiled; no ultrasonic.
