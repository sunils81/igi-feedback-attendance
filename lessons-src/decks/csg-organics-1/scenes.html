Natural, cultured or imitation
Natural

No human help

Forms around an irritant or displaced tissue inside the mollusc, coated with nacre over years. Very rare today. Example: Basra pearls from the Persian Gulf.

Cultured

Human started

A bead and/or mantle tissue is implanted; the mollusc then coats it with nacre. Kept about 6 months to 2–3 years. Examples: Akoya (Japan, China), Tahitian, South Sea, freshwater.

Imitation

Not a pearl

Glass, plastic or shell beads coated with a pearly lacquer. Smooth and gritless on the teeth.

From implant to harvest · step through

Under X-ray · inner structure

X-radiography is the key test; a lab can also use micro-CT. A drill hole lets you see a bead’s boundary with a loupe.

Momme
1 grain = 0.25 ct (4 grains = 1 carat). 1 momme = 3.75 g = 18.75 ct, used in the Japanese trade for lots of Akoya pearls.
Mabe, keshi, baroque and mother-of-pearl

Mabe

An assembled blister pearl: nacre dome, filler and mother-of-pearl backing.

Keshi

Nacre-only by-products of culturing, formed when the bead is rejected. Can also form naturally.

Baroque

Irregular, free-form pearls.

Mother-of-pearl

The nacreous lining of the shell itself.

Pearl treatments and care

Bleaching

To whiten and even out colour. Very common in Akoya and freshwater pearls.

Dyeing

For fashion colours or “black” pearls. Colour concentrates in drill holes and blemishes.

Silver nitrate

An older method that turns white pearls grey to black.

Pearls are soft and porous. Keep them away from perfume, cosmetics and acids; put them on last, and wipe them after wearing.
What we learned

Three types

Natural (no help), cultured (bead and/or tissue implanted), imitation (coated beads).

Identify

X-ray: natural shows rings to the centre; bead-cultured shows a banded bead.

Sort

Size, quality, colour and overtone, shape. 1 grain = 0.25 ct.
