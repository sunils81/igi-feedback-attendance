Better the colour, higher the price · same variety, different colour

The sweet spot

A pure hue, medium to medium-dark tone and strong saturation. Too light or too dark both lower the price.

Transparency, and the extra value of phenomena

Transparent beats translucent

Of the same variety, transparent stones command a higher price than translucent or opaque ones.

Phenomena add value

A sharp, centred star or eye, or a strong colour change, raises the price.

Opaque gems are judged on overall appearance

Look for

Even, attractive colour, a good polish, and pattern (banding, matrix) that suits the market.

Size · the price per carat climbs with weight

0.50–3.00 ct is widely available. 3 ct and above is a premium size. Illustrative curve only, not market prices.
Cut and shape

Good cut

Even colour face-up, few dark or washed-out areas (windows), good symmetry and polish.

Shape demand

Ovals and cushions are the commonest for coloured stones; rounds and some fancy shapes may carry a premium.

Coloured stones are often cut to save weight. A deep belly adds carats but not beauty.
Origin · natural, untreated, premium size and quality, with a famous name

Common gems

Citrine, aquamarine, tourmaline and garnet are plentiful, so even fine ones cost far less.

Any treatment lowers value · synthetics sell for far less

What we learned

Quality

Colour first, then transparency. Opaque gems: overall appearance.

Size, cut, origin

Price per carat rises with size; 3 ct+ is premium. Classic origins add value.

Treatment and synthetics

Untreated stones are worth more; synthetics sell for a fraction.
