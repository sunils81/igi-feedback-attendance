Reflection · refraction · total internal reflection

Light going from a denser to a rarer medium bends away from the normal. Beyond the critical angle none escapes: total internal reflection, the principle of the refractometer.
Construction and set-up

Light

White light works, but the standard is monochromatic yellow, about 589.3 nm (sodium light).

Contact liquid

Makes optical contact between stone and glass. Its RI (about 1.79) is the upper limit: stones above it read “over the limit”.

Care

The glass is soft and scratches easily: watch metal mounts. Too much liquid gives a dark ring. Good polish and a flat facet give a sharp edge.

Reading the shadow edge

The shadow edge sits between the 1.54 and 1.55 bars, just above the middle.

Estimate the third decimal

Just above half-way adds 0.004, so the reading is 1.544: quartz.

Spot reading for cabochons

Curved stones give no edge. Use a tiny spot of liquid and the distant-vision method: the spot changes from light to dark at its RI, here about 1.54.

How light travels through the stone

Isotropic · SR

Light travels at the same speed in every direction. Cubic crystals and amorphous materials (glass). One constant reading.

Uniaxial · DR

Light splits into two rays. Tetragonal, hexagonal and trigonal. One constant + one variable reading.

Biaxial · DR

Light splits into two rays. Orthorhombic, monoclinic and triclinic. Two variable readings.

Birefringence is the difference between the highest and lowest readings. Example: 1.619 and 1.612 gives 0.007.

What we learned

Principle

Total internal reflection beyond the critical angle. Yellow 589.3 nm light, 1.79 contact liquid.

Reading

Shadow edge to three decimals; spot reading for cabochons.

Optic character

SR: one fixed edge. Uniaxial: one fixed, one moving. Biaxial: two moving. Birefringence = high − low.
