The Spectroscope

A gem’s colour is what is left after it absorbs part of the light. The missing parts appear as dark lines and bands, a fingerprint for many stones.

Visible light, 400–700 nm · the stone keeps some colours and removes others

Absorption spectrum

Dark lines (narrow) and bands (broad) where light is absorbed. Many are typical of a particular gem.

Colouring elements

Chromium, iron, titanium, copper, manganese, vanadium, cobalt… each leaves its own pattern.

Two types

Prism: bright, uneven scale. Diffraction grating: even scale, dimmer.

Viewing with a hand-held spectroscope

Transmitted light

Strong white light through the stone (or reflected off it) into the slit, in a dark room.

Rough or polished

Works on both, loose or set.

Best for

Ruby, almandine, pyrope, rhodolite, zircon, peridot, emerald, cobalt-blue synthetics.

What we learned

Principle

Selective absorption leaves dark lines and bands; colouring elements cause them.

Instruments

Prism (bright, uneven scale) or diffraction grating (even scale). Strong transmitted light.

Key spectra

Ruby, almandine 505/527/576, zircon 653.5, peridot 453/473/493, cobalt spinel three bands.
