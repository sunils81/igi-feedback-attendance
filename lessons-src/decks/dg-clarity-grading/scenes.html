Weighing the evidence

Finding inclusions is half the job. The other half is judging how much they matter.

Five parameters for clarity grading

Every characteristic is weighed on five things

1

Nature

What it is. A feather can spread; a pinpoint cannot.

2

Size

The larger it is, the easier it is to see.

3

Number

One small crystal, or dozens.

4

Position

Under the table it shows most; near the girdle it hides.

5

Colour · relief

Dark or high-contrast features stand out.

Flaws: two families

Inclusions set clarity. Blemishes set polish and symmetry.

Making the call

Find the grade-setter

1 · See everything

Look face-up, through the pavilion and in profile until you have found every characteristic.

2 · Find the grade-setter

The one inclusion, or group, that matters most once the five parameters are weighed.

3 · Call it face-up

The grade depends on how the inclusions look face-up, because that is how the diamond is worn.

The top of the scale
FL · Flawless

No inclusions and no blemishes visible to the trained eye at 10x.

To earn FL at IGI

  • Polish and symmetry must both be Excellent
  • The girdle must be faceted, polished or bruted cleanly
  • A natural diamond with strong fluorescence is not given FL
IF · Internally flawless

No inclusions at 10x. Only blemishes are allowed.

One inclusion, many images

Reflections count, but are never plotted

The facets act like mirrors and repeat an inclusion across the stone. Face-up, those reflections make it look bigger, so they are considered in the grade.

On the plot, draw only the real inclusion, never its reflections.

Section 03

Plotting

A plot is a map of the stone’s characteristics. It is drawn after the grade is decided.

What, why and how

What is plotting?

Diagramming the characteristics of a diamond on a crown and pavilion outline. Always done after clarity grading.

Why plot?

Mainly for identification: no two plots are alike. It also supports the clarity grade.

How?

Only two colours: inclusions in red, blemishes in green.

The hardest part · which side?

Everything is plotted on the crown…

Unless

It is only seen from the pavilion

Unless

It touches or breaks the pavilion surface

And

If it breaks both crown and pavilion, plot it on both

Orientation · think of the stone as a clock

Put each inclusion where it really is

Hold the stone in a fixed orientation and describe positions like a clock face: a feather at four o’clock near the girdle, a crystal at ten o’clock under the table.

Keep the same orientation

When you turn the stone over for the pavilion, 3 and 9 swap sides on the diagram. Check every inclusion against both views.

Draw the shape and size

A long feather is drawn as a long line in the right direction, not as a dot.

Laser inscription for identification

Check the girdle in profile

Many diamonds carry a laser inscription on the girdle, usually a report number. It is not a characteristic and is not plotted.

Record it in Comments

Write Pre-Existing Laserscribe On Girdle and the message, for example:

PELOG ‘IGI 6548’

What we learned

Weigh, then call

Nature, size, number, position, colour. Find the grade-setter. Call the grade face-up.

Eleven grades

FL, IF, VVS1–2, VS1–2, SI1–2, I1–3. I grades are judged by light blockage and naked-eye visibility.

Plot after grading

Red inclusions, green blemishes. Crown unless pavilion-only or breaking the pavilion. Never plot reflections.

Inscriptions

Note them in Comments as PELOG with the message.
