01
Section 01

Tools & lighting

Clarity is graded with a 10x loupe under standard light. The same tools and the same light every time make grades repeatable.

Tools used for clarity grading

Everything on the grading desk

10x loupe

Triplet, fully corrected. The standard for every clarity grade.

Tweezers

Hold the stone securely by the girdle.

Gem cloth & sorting pad

Clean the stone first: dust and grease look like inclusions.

Diffused white light

Fluorescent, UV-corrected standard daylight.

No polishing lenses, and no loupe stronger than 10x. Higher magnification shows things the grade is not based on.
The ideal position

Steady hands, steady image

  • Loupe held close to the eye, about 2.5 cm from the stone
  • The hand with the tweezers rests against the hand with the loupe
  • Elbows on the desk to stop any shake
  • Both eyes open, to avoid strain over a long session
Two lighting techniques

Where you hold the stone changes what you see

Darkfield lighting

Tweezers at the edge of the light source. Light enters from the side, the background stays dark, and inclusions and blemishes glow. Use it to find characteristics.

Reflected lighting

Tweezers inside the light. Light bounces off the facets so surface marks show as breaks in the reflection. Use it to confirm whether a feature is on the surface.

Three positions to view a diamond
1

Face-up · crown

Looking down through the table. The clarity grade is decided face-up.

2

Pavilion view

Turned over. Tiny inclusions often hide until seen from below.

3

Profile · side

Through the girdle: depth of inclusions, bearded girdle, naturals, inscriptions.

02
Section 02

Inclusions

Any internal characteristic is an inclusion. How important and how visible they are at 10x decides the clarity grade.

Inclusion 1 · FTR

Feather

A crack or fracture inside the stone. Seen side-on it is a thin line; turned, it shows a white, feathery plane. A feather that reaches the surface can be a durability risk.

Inclusions 2–4 · crystals and points
INX

Included crystal

A mineral crystal trapped as the diamond grew. Often colourless, sometimes another diamond.

PP

Pinpoint

A crystal so small it looks like a single dot at 10x.

GPP

Group of pinpoints

Several pinpoints close together, each still visible on its own.

Inclusions 5–7
CLD

Cloud

Pinpoints packed too closely to see one by one: a hazy, milky patch.

K

Knot

An included crystal that reaches the surface. It may show as a raised area under reflected light.

DINX

Dark included crystal

A black or dark crystal. Very visible, so it weighs heavily on the grade.

Inclusion 8 · LDH · a treatment

Laser drill hole

A laser burns a fine tunnel from the surface to a dark inclusion, which is then bleached or dissolved with acid. The tunnel stays.

Plotted in two colours

The tunnel inside is red; the opening on the surface is green.

Laser drilling is a treatment and must be disclosed. More on Day 17.

03
Section 03

Blemishes

Any characteristic confined to the surface is a blemish. Blemishes mostly affect the polish and symmetry grades.

Surface marks · polish-related
Don’t confuse them

Burn mark vs cloud

Burn mark · on the surface

Disappears in reflected light as a break in the facet’s shine. Can be removed by re-polishing.

Cloud · inside the stone

Stays visible from every angle and does not touch the facet reflection. It is an inclusion and affects clarity.

Left from the rough or added by the cutter
N

Natural

A piece of the original crystal skin left unpolished, usually on the girdle. It may show trigons or growth lines. In lab-grown diamonds it is called a growth remnant.

EF

Extra facet

A facet that is not part of the cutting style, often polished to remove a natural or a nick near the girdle.

04
Section 04

Structural phenomena

Irregularities caused by deformation of the atomic structure while the crystal grew.

Three structural phenomena
EXT

External twinning

Twin lines that show on the surface as fine grooves crossing facet junctions.

INT

Internal twinning

Twin planes inside the stone, seen as reflective lines or wisps.

IG · plotted

Internal graining

Lines or bands from uneven growth. It counts as an inclusion and is the only structural feature plotted.

Coming up tomorrow · weighing what you see

The five parameters of clarity grading

1

Nature

What it is: a feather is riskier than a pinpoint.

2

Size

Bigger is more visible.

3

Number

One or many.

4

Position

Under the table is worst; near the girdle is easier to hide.

5

Colour · relief

Dark, high-contrast features stand out.

What we learned

Tools and light

10x loupe, tweezers, gem cloth, diffused daylight. Darkfield to find, reflected light to confirm.

Inclusions are inside

FTR, INX, PP, GPP, CLD, K, DINX, LDH, BG, CV, C, IN, PM, CC. They set the clarity grade.

Blemishes are on the surface

Nick, scratch, polish lines, burn marks, abrasions, natural, extra facet. They affect polish and symmetry.

Structural

Twinning and graining; only internal graining is plotted.
