Section 01

Every colour exists

Diamonds occur naturally in every colour. Pure carbon in a perfect lattice is colourless; colour comes from what disturbs it.

Three causes of colour
1

Lattice irregularities

Structural changes and distortion of the crystal lattice.

BrownPinkRed
2

Impurities

Atoms of another element inside the lattice. Nitrogen gives yellow; boron gives blue.

3

Natural radiation

Exposure to radiation in the Earth’s crust gives natural green.

Green
Tools used
  • White, non-reflecting colour card (shade card)
  • Tweezers and gem cloth
  • Diamond master stones: a pre-certified comparison set
  • Diffused white fluorescent light, UV-corrected standard daylight
Colour is graded with the naked eye, judging the intensity of colour in the belly of the stone, not its body colour.

Fancy colours

Below the Y-Z master, or any strong hue, a diamond is graded as a fancy colour, face-up.

Fancy colour gallery
Brown diamonds and their trade names

Champagne

Light browns. Argyle’s scale starts here at C1. In D–Z terms they sit around S to Z.

Cognac

Deeper orange-browns, the dark end of the Argyle scale. Graded as fancy colour.

Chocolate

A marketing name for dark brown diamonds. On a report the grade is still a fancy brown.

Grading with masters

Estimate by eye, check fluorescence, then compare with master stones and confirm.

Step 1 · Visual estimation (VE)

Pavilion first, then the crown

Does it look colourless, slightly coloured, or obviously coloured? Ask it twice: once table-down, once table-up.

Lower the contrast, higher the colour. Higher contrast between the views means less colour.

Criteria for ideal colour masters

Every master sits at the top of its range

A G master marks the best G. A stone that looks the same as the G master is a high G; slightly darker is still G.

D (0+)E (0)F (1+)G (1)H (2)I (3)J (4)… Z (20)
  • Natural diamonds, always
  • Round brilliants
  • Clarity SI1 or better
  • Fluorescence: none
  • Colourless to yellow only
  • Girdles faceted, or at least polished
  • Cut: Very Good
  • Weight 50–80 cents: big stones can be compared with small masters, not the reverse

Check fluorescence before using masters

Fluorescence

Visible light given off when a diamond is exposed to short-wavelength energy such as X-rays or ultraviolet. For colour grading we use long-wave UV. Blue is the most common colour.

None · NVery slight · VSLSlight · SLStrong · STRVery strong · VSTR

How to check

Pavilion side up, stone about ½ inch above the shelf of the UV box. It shows instantly, so judge quickly.

One extra step for strong fluorescence

Compare face-up too

Looks better than the master?

Upgrade by one grade, for example H to G.

Same or darker?

Keep the grade.

K and below

Strong fluorescence can make these look darker. Don’t upgrade or downgrade.

Points to remember at the colour table
  • Do the visual estimation before using masters
  • Check fluorescence before starting with masters
  • Never skip the confirmatory step
  • Don’t let the two stones touch: colour seems to “transfer”
  • Never more than two stones in the tray
  • Only one master out at a time
  • Check the plot before leaving the table: is it the same stone?
  • Never carry a master stone away from the table
What we learned

Causes

Lattice distortion (brown, pink, red), nitrogen (yellow), boron (blue), radiation (green).

The scale

D–F colourless, G–J near colourless, K–M faint, N–Z very light to light. Other hues are named: Faint, Light, Tinge, Very Light.

Fancy colours

Graded face-up by hue, tone and saturation. The last word is the dominant hue.

Procedure

VE pavilion then crown → fluorescence → master on the left → confirm on the right.
