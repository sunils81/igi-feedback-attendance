Fancy Shapes

Any shape other than the round. Same diamond, same 4Cs, but new names, new measurements and a few grading habits of their own.

Definition

Any shape other than round.

Fancy shapes are manufactured depending on two conditions:

1

The rough crystal

The shape that gives the maximum yield from that particular piece of rough.

2

Market demand

What buyers want this season sells best, so cutters follow it.

Measuring & weight

Length, width and height, the length-to-width ratio, and a formula for each shape to estimate carat weight.

Measurements
L

Length

The vertical measurement, point to point or end to end.

W

Width

The horizontal measurement, across the belly. It is the 100% figure.

H

Height

From the table to the culet.

Total depth % = (height ÷ width) × 100
L/W ratio = length ÷ width, written as “1.75 : 1”

Clarity & colour

Clarity and plotting work exactly as for rounds. Colour needs one extra habit: always grade fancy shapes through the pavilion.

Fancy shapes look one to two grades darker face-up · compare with round masters

Girdle and culet

Girdle

  • Estimated the same way as rounds: EXTN, VTN, TN, MED, SLTK, TK, VTK, EXTK
  • Heart: ignore the thickness at the cleft, judge the rest
  • Curved outlines have a faceted girdle; flat-edged shapes have long facets

Culet

  • Brilliant fancies: pointed, none, abraded, chipped, faceted, natural
  • Faceted and natural culets: state the size
  • Emerald (step cut): the elongated culet is the keel line, recorded as “Long”, or “Long facet” if faceted
Points: French tip vs bezel (regular) tip

Regular (bezel) tip

A bezel facet runs right out to the point.

French tip

Star and upper-girdle facets meet at the point instead, a look many buyers like on pears, hearts and marquises.

Points are the weakest part of the stone: always check them for chips.

What we learned

Shapes

Modified brilliants, step cuts, rose cut and uncut. Cut for yield and for demand.

Measuring

L, W (the 100% figure), H. TD% = H ÷ W × 100. The L/W ratio picks the weight factor.

Colour

Through the pavilion, diagonal position, against round masters.

Cut

Bow tie (face-up), bulge (profile), outline symmetry per shape.
