Introduction to jewellery

From the Old French “jouel” and the Latin “jocale”: a plaything. Spelled “jewellery” in British English and “jewelry” in American English.

Fine or fashion?
Fine jewellery

Made with precious materials such as gold, platinum, diamonds and pearls.

Fashion jewellery

Made from inexpensive materials such as brass or steel.

Who is a jeweller, and what do they owe?

To customers

  • Offer a variety of designs, with full details of each piece
  • Disclose the materials at the time of sale
  • Hallmarked metal; diamond and gemstone weights stated
  • A bill with every sale
  • Explain terms of sale, exchange and return
  • Check quality and finish before sale
  • Provide the after-sales service promised

To employees

  • A safe working environment
  • Clear terms in a written contract
  • Proper training for the role
  • Pay on time
  • Hear and address grievances

Section 03

Precious metals

Rare, chemically stable, resistant to corrosion, beautiful, and malleable and ductile: they can be beaten into sheet and drawn into wire.

Why we alloy

Too soft when pure

Alloying (melting two or more metals together) gives the hardness needed for jewellery and lowers the melting point. Two metals: binary alloy; three: ternary.

To change colour

White gold, rose gold. Common alloy metals: silver, copper, zinc, palladium.

Karat = parts in 24

18k = 18 parts pure gold out of 24 (75%). 24k ≈ 99.99%. 18k or lower is used for studded jewellery: harder, holds stones better.

Silver, platinum and others

Silver

Softer than gold or platinum; tarnishes without care. Pure 99.9%; sterling .925 (92.5% + copper); coin silver 90%. Non-tarnish alloys (Argentium, germanium silver) keep at least 92.5%.

Platinum

The rarest of the three; dense and hard-wearing. Pt 900 = 90%, Pt 950 = 95%. To be called platinum it must be 90–95% pure.

Other metals

Titanium, steel, aluminium and tungsten for contemporary and men’s jewellery.

Worked examples from your notes

Purity of 14k

14 × 100 ÷ 24 = 58.333%

Purity of 21k

21 × 100 ÷ 24 = 87.5%

Pure gold in a 5.500 g 18k ring

5.500 × 18 ÷ 24 = 4.125 g

Exchange: 10 g of 14k for 8.250 g earrings in 18k

10 × 14 ÷ 18 = 7.778 g of 18k. Needed 8.250 − 7.778 = 0.472 g more 18k (0.354 g pure).

Cost of gold: 3.600 g ring in 18k at $70/g

3.600 × 18 ÷ 24 = 2.700 g × $70 = $189
