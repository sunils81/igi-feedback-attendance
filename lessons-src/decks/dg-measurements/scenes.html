Section 01

Life, fire, sparkle

Three things make a polished diamond beautiful: brilliance, dispersion and scintillation. All three depend on how it is cut.

Factors responsible for beauty
“Life”

Brilliance

White light reflected back out through the table.

“Fire”

Dispersion

White light split into rainbow colours by the crown facets.

“Sparkle”

Scintillation

Flashes of light as the stone, the light or the eye moves, created by the facets.

The Tolkowsky ideal cut · 1919

A balance of brilliance and fire

Marcel Tolkowsky, a mathematician from a family of Antwerp cutters, calculated the angles that balance brilliance and dispersion.

Table53%Crown angle34.5°Pavilion angle40.75°Crown height16.2%Pavilion depth43.1%
The catch: low weight retention. Cutters lose more of the rough to reach these proportions.

Measuring a round

Machines measure a stone in seconds. A grader still needs to measure it by hand with a millimetre gauge.

Measuring machines

InstrumentsDiaMension, DiaScan, DiaScan HDSoftwareDiaVision

The stone turns on a stage while a camera records its outline, building every diameter, height and angle.

Average girdle diameter

The 100% figure for rounds

With a millimetre gauge take 6 to 8 readings across the girdle. Keep the smallest and the largest.

AGD = Min + Max2

Example: min 6.48 mm, max 6.54 mm → AGD = 6.51 mm.

Height and total depth %

Height: from table to culet

Measured with the millimetre gauge. Total depth % compares it with the AGD:

T.D.% = (Height ÷ AGD) × 100

Girdle & culet

The girdle is the plane dividing the crown from the pavilion, with 16 valleys and 16 peaks. The culet is where the pavilion ends.

Girdle visualiser · profile view, reflected light, 10x

How the girdle is written on a report

Minimum – maximum

Most girdles vary around the stone, so give both ends.

TN – MED
VTN – SLTK

Uniform girdle

If it is the same all round, give one thickness.

MED

Plus the finish

Add how the girdle is finished, if not bruted.

TN – MED (FAC)
TN – MED (Partly POL)

Cutting style of the girdle

Bruted

Waxy or frosty looking.

Faceted · FAC

Individual small facets all around the girdle.

Polished · POL

Buffed and shiny all around, done on a rounding machine.

Partly FAC · Partly POL

Faceted or polished only in some areas, not all the way round.

When a characteristic sits on the girdle

Judge the girdle that is really there

Contained in the girdle

A natural, cavity, chip or indented natural fully inside the girdle: ignore it and judge the thickness.

Takes up part of it

A natural, extra facet, cavity, chip or indented natural over part of the girdle: judge the remaining part.

Covers it entirely

A natural, extra facet, cavity or indented natural across the whole valley: don’t judge thickness in that valley.

What we learned

Beauty

Brilliance (life), dispersion (fire), scintillation (sparkle). Tolkowsky balanced them in 1919.

Measuring

AGD = (min + max) ÷ 2. TD% = height ÷ AGD × 100.

Weight

1 ct = 0.2 g = 100 points. Estimate: AGD² × H × 0.0061, plus 2–7% for thick girdles.

Girdle and culet

EXTN to EXTK, written min – max with finish. Culet: pointed, none, abraded, chipped, faceted, natural.
