The loupe mm gauge

A 10x lens at one end, a millimetre scale at the other. Hold the piece very close, put 0 on one edge of the girdle and read across the diameter.

Each millimetre is divided into 10 parts: 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 … 1.9 mm.

Colour grading in jewellery

A great challenge

The metal colours the stones. Grade in profile view, at the edge of the light source. If they show too much colour face-up, the grade is K or below.

Ignore the metal

Yellow metal reflects into the diamonds and can make them look a grade better. Concentrate only on the diamonds.

Identifying imitations in jewellery · it takes more expertise

Look for

  • Transparent girdles
  • Severely scratched surfaces
  • Abraded facet junctions

Moissanite

Strong dispersion and doubling of back facet edges are the best observations.

Remember

Thermal testers can’t separate moissanite. Weight is estimated, not weighed, while stones are set.

What we learned

Measure

Loupe mm gauge: 0 on one edge, read across, to 0.1 mm.

Clarity

Up to 20x if needed. Split grades; VVS/VS needs 60–70% VVS.

Colour

Profile view at the edge of the light. Ignore metal reflections. EF, GH, IJ, JK…

Cut and imitations

Replace fish eyes and nail heads. Transparent girdles, abraded edges, doubling.
