Section 01

Standardisation

The trade prices polished diamonds from a common list, then agrees a discount or premium for each stone.

The Rapaport sheet

List price × (1 − discount)

Reading the grid

Each grid is a weight range. Rows are colour, columns are clarity. Values are in hundreds of US dollars per carat.

“Back” means discount

“25 back” = 25% below the list. A premium is above the list.

What moves the market

Global events, demand, and online trading platforms (RapNet) where dealers list stones by the 4Cs.

Certification

A recognised laboratory report removes doubt about the grades, so certified stones trade with more confidence and often at better prices.

Auction houses

A powerful influence on major stones

Sotheby’s and Christie’s began regular sales of important diamonds and coloured stones in the 1970s. Their results set the price of rare goods.

A record that stood 20 years

In 1987 the 0.95 ct Hancock Red sold at Christie’s New York for more than $926,000 per carat.

What we learned

The list

Rapaport: hundreds of USD per carat. Price = list × (1 − discount).

Discounts

Bigger, cleaner, whiter, better cut = smaller discount. Magic weights and H&A earn premiums.

Seller’s market

Rough + manufacturing + certification + margin for goods off the list.
