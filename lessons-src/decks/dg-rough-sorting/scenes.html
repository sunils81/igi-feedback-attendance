Section 01

Every stone is checked

A mine produces a mixture of everything from gem crystals to industrial grit. Sorting turns that mixture into parcels that can be valued and sold.

The sorting room

The stone must be thoroughly checked

Four questions for every stone

Sorters judge each crystal on four criteria

1

Size

Sieve size, or carat weight for larger stones.

2

Model

The shape of the crystal: what it can be cut into.

3

Quality

Clarity: how many inclusions, spots or cracks, and where.

4

Colour

From colourless through yellows and browns to fancy colours.

Size first · sieves

A stack of plates with round holes

Rough is shaken through a stack of sieve plates, the largest holes on top. Each stone stops on the first plate whose holes are too small for it to pass.

The plate a stone stops on gives its sieve size. Larger stones, above the sieve range, are weighed and sorted one by one.

03
Section 03

Gem categories

The category tells the manufacturer how the stone will be cut, and so roughly what it will yield.

Sawables

Well-formed crystals worth sawing in two

Sawables are regular crystals, usually octahedra or dodecahedra, that can be sawn across the middle to yield two polished stones.

Within one category

Sawables of different colour, and different quality

Sawable long · spotted

Sawable long

Elongated sawable crystals: still worth sawing, but the planner must allow for the stretched shape.

Spotted

Good shapes, but with dark spots (inclusions). The number, size and position of the spots decide the grade.

Makeables

Polished in one piece

Makeables are irregular crystals that are not worth sawing. They are polished as a single stone, following the crystal’s own shape.

Sawable or makeable? Ask: would cutting it in two give two good stones? If not, it is a makeable.
Macles & twins

Flat, triangular twins get their own category

Macles are thin, so they suit flatter shapes such as triangles and hearts. Regular penetration twins are sorted with them.

Irradiated and frosted

Irradiated

Natural radiation stains: green or brown spots or skins. Usually only skin-deep.

Frosted

A matt, etched skin that hides the inside until a window is polished.

Cleavages and flats

Cleavage

Irregular or broken pieces, often with cracks or inclusions. They are cleaved (split along a cleavage plane) to remove the flawed part before polishing.

Flats

Thin, flat crystals. Like macles, they suit shallow, spread-out shapes.

Section 04

Industrial quality

A large share of every mine’s output is not gem quality. Its hardness still makes it the most useful abrasive there is.

Boart and carbonado

Too flawed to polish, too hard to waste

Boart (bort) is poorly crystallised, heavily included or dark rough. It is crushed into grit and powder for cutting, grinding and polishing, including polishing other diamonds.
