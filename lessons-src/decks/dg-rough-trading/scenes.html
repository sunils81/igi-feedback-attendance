Buying at the source

Much of Africa’s and South America’s rough is found by individual diggers in rivers. It is bought stone by stone, often in a village office.

Buying offices around the world
What the buyer is doing

A buying office makes the first valuation

1

Weigh

On a balance, sometimes with improvised weights such as matches when there is no scale.

2

Look

Loupe in hand: shape, colour, cracks and inclusions, exactly the sorting criteria.

3

Negotiate

The digger and the buyer agree a price, stone by stone or parcel by parcel.

4

Certify

Exported rough needs a Kimberley Process certificate from the government.

Section 03

The sight system

For most of the 20th century De Beers controlled most of the world’s rough. It sold it in sealed boxes, to invited buyers, at events called sights.

How the system worked

One channel for most of the world’s rough

Rough from De Beers’ own mines, partner mines and contract purchases was pooled in London, sorted, valued and sold, while De Beers marketed diamonds to consumers (“A Diamond is Forever”).

Names changed over time: the CSO (Central Selling Organisation) became the DTC (Diamond Trading Company), today De Beers Global Sightholder Sales. Since 2013 sights are held in Gaborone, Botswana.
Preparing a sight

From the scale to the sightholder

What a sightholder is

An invited buyer with a box waiting

Invited, not open

Only approved companies, usually large manufacturers and dealers, are invited to buy.

Take the box as offered

Each box is a mix chosen by the seller. Traditionally the buyer accepted it at the set price or declined the whole box.

Ten sights a year

Sales are held at regular intervals through the year, which keeps rough flowing to the cutting centres.

Section 04

Bourses & centres

A diamond bourse is a members-only trading hall where deals worth millions are closed with a handshake and the words “mazal u’bracha”.

Antwerp · the No. 1 diamond centre for centuries

One square mile of diamonds

Buyers from all over the world come to the Antwerp diamond district to buy rough and polished. It is home to four bourses, including a dedicated rough bourse, the Diamond Kring.

IGI was founded here in 1975; its first laboratory is in the Antwerp diamond district.
Surat · the world’s largest cutting centre

Where most diamonds are polished

India cuts and polishes the large majority of the world’s diamonds by number of stones, and most of that work happens in Surat.

The Surat Diamond Bourse, opened in 2023, is one of the largest office buildings in the world. Mumbai’s Bharat Diamond Bourse in BKC is India’s main trading hub.

The Rapaport sheet

A shared starting point for prices

Published by Martin Rapaport since 1978, the sheet lists asking prices for polished diamonds by shape, size, colour and clarity.

How it reads

Prices are in hundreds of US dollars per carat. A box showing 50 means $5,000 per carat.

How the trade uses it

Deals are quoted as a discount or premium to the list, for example “Rap minus 20%”. We will use it on the pricing day.

The last link in the chain

Polished stones are graded before they are sold

Buyers trade on a laboratory report that records weight, colour, clarity, cut and measurements. Learning to grade like a lab is what the rest of this course is about.
