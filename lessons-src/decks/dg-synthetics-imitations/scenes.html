Three very different things
Natural diamond

Grown in the Earth

Carbon crystallised deep in the mantle over billions of years.

Lab-grown diamond

Real diamond, made by people

Same carbon crystal, same hardness and optics. Also sold as synthetic, man-made, created, cultured or “eco-friendly”.

Imitation · simulant

Looks like diamond

A different material: moissanite, CZ, GGG, YAG, glass…

01
Section 01

Growing diamonds

Two methods make gem-quality diamonds today: HPHT, which copies the mantle’s pressure and heat, and CVD, which builds diamond from a gas.

HPHT · high pressure, high temperature

A press, a capsule, a seed

1

Load the capsule

Carbon (graphite), a metal catalyst and a tiny diamond seed go into the growth chamber.

2

Squeeze and heat

The metal melts, dissolves the carbon, and diamond crystallises on the seed.

3

Harvest

After about four days the crystal is removed. Presses: the BARS (traditional) and the belt type (outdated).

What HPHT diamonds show

Crystal shape

Cube + octahedron faces, unlike natural octahedra.

Metallic flux

Shiny metal inclusions from the catalyst. Common in BARS stones.

Magnetic

With metallic inclusions, a stone can be picked up by a strong magnet.

Phosphorescence

Many glow on after the UV is switched off.

Colourless is now routine
0.02 ct

A colourless BARS HPHT lab-grown diamond

HPHT was long known for small yellow stones. Today large, colourless gem-quality crystals are grown routinely.

CVD · chemical vapour deposition

Diamond from a gas, layer by layer

Methane and hydrogen are fed into a vacuum chamber. Microwaves turn them into a glowing plasma; carbon atoms rain down and build diamond on thin seed plates, one layer at a time.

NPD (nano-polycrystalline diamond) is a newer material made from industrial-grade diamond; it shows a pink, roiled pattern in DiamondView.

Section 02

Every stone is screened

A lab-grown diamond passes every simple test for diamond. Telling it from natural needs laboratory instruments.

Laboratory procedure · step 1: screening

Every polished diamond must be screened

Screening devices check how the stone absorbs light. Most natural diamonds pass; anything else is referred for further tests.

Step 2 · DiamondView: how the crystal grew

Ultraviolet makes each growth zone glow differently, revealing the pattern of growth.

Small stones need screening too
0

diamonds in a 15.32 ct parcel. One was found to be lab-grown under the photoluminescence machine.

IGI issues lab-grown diamond reports with full disclosure, and inscribes “LABORATORY GROWN” on the girdle.

Section 03

Imitations

Synthetic moissanite, CZ, GGG, YAG, strontium titanate, synthetic sapphire and spinel, zircon, topaz, quartz, glass. A few simple tests catch them.

Physical properties

Facet edges · hardness

Diamond (10) keeps razor-sharp edges. Strontium titanate (5–6) and zircon (7.5) show abraded, rounded junctions.

Breath (huff) test

Breathe on it: the fog vanishes almost instantly from diamond, which has the highest thermal inertia. Diamond testers use the same property, but synthetic moissanite tests positive, so use a multi-tester.

Heat test

Moissanite turns yellow when heated and returns to colourless as it cools. Diamond stays colourless.

Inclusions, girdles and culets

A polished girdle on an imitation is usually transparent; a polished diamond girdle stays opaque. A faceted girdle is always diamond.

Read-through test · well-cut round brilliants only

Place the stone table down on a line or newsprint. Light leaks from the pavilion of most imitations, so you see the line.

Can’t see the line

Diamond · synthetic moissanite · strontium titanate

Can see the line

CZ · GGG · YAG · synthetic spinel

Not for fancy shapes or deep pavilions.
Doubling of back facet edges · diagonal face-up, darkfield, 10x

Single vs double refraction

Diamond is singly refracting (RI 2.417). Doubly refracting stones split light in two, so edges look doubled.

What we learned

Lab-grown is diamond

HPHT (press, metal flux, seed) and CVD (plasma, layers on seed plates).

Detection needs a lab

Screen every stone; DiamondView shows layered (natural), hourglass (HPHT), curved striations (CVD).

Imitations fail simple tests

Edges, huff, heat, heft, inclusions, girdle; dispersion, read-through, doubling.

Moissanite

Passes a thermal tester: check doubling, stringers and heat.
