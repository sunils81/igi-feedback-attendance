Section 01 · clarity treatment · unstable

Fracture filling

Glass is forced into surface-reaching feathers so they disappear. Also called glass filling (GF). It is not permanent.

Why a filled crack vanishes

An open feather holds air, which reflects light and shows white. Glass with a refractive index close to diamond’s lets the light pass, so the feather becomes nearly invisible.

Detecting a filling under magnification
How to see it

Tilt the stone and watch the colours shift

Changes with the light

Moving between darkfield and the direct light of the microscope changes the colour of the flashes.

Look from the other side

The flash is often best seen looking almost parallel to the plane of the filled feather, from the opposite side.

Natural look-alikes in untreated diamonds

Iron oxide stains

Brown-orange patches of colour inside natural, untreated cracks.

Radiation stains

Green spots. Report comment: Indication of residual natural irradiation.

Rainbow feather

Natural iridescence in an open feather. The colours don’t change like a flash effect.

Why filling is unstable

The filling can be damaged by

Ultrasonic cleaningSteam cleaningDirect heatingAcid boiling
A jeweller’s torch during repair can boil the glass out. Customers must be told, and told how to care for the stone.
02
Section 02 · clarity treatment · stable

Laser drilling

A laser reaches a dark inclusion so it can be bleached or burned away. Permanent, but always disclosed.

External laser drill · LDH

A tunnel from the surface

The laser drills a fine channel from the surface to the dark crystal. Acid boiled into the channel bleaches or dissolves it.

Internal laser drill · ILD · also called KM treatment

No hole, just new feathers

The laser heats the dark inclusion until tiny feathers open from it to the surface. Acid then enters through these and bleaches the crystal.

What to look for

Irregular, worm-like or step-like feathers leading from a whitened inclusion to the surface.

Report comment: Presence of Internal Laser.

Section 03

Natural or artificial colour?

Every fancy colour can be produced or improved by treatment. Only a laboratory can tell for certain.

Types of colour treatment
Unstable

Coating

A thin film on the pavilion adds or masks colour. It can wear or be cleaned off.

Stable

HPHT

High pressure and high temperature remove brown from type IIa diamonds, making them colourless, or create pink, blue, yellow or green.

Stable

Irradiation

Radiation, often followed by heating (annealing), produces greens, blues, yellows, pinks and reds.

Diamond types · by nitrogen and boron

Type I has nitrogen. Type II has almost none.

Type Ia

Ia

Nitrogen in clusters. Most natural diamonds; colourless to yellow (“Cape”).

Type Ib

Ib

Single nitrogen atoms. Rare in nature; intense yellow to orange. Typical of HPHT synthetics.

Type IIa

IIa

Practically no nitrogen. Colourless, brown or pink. Brown IIa can be made colourless by HPHT.

Type IIb

IIb

Contains boron. Blue, and conducts electricity.

HPHT colour modification of type II diamonds

The GE POL story

In 1999 the first commercial HPHT-processed diamonds, brownish type IIa stones made colourless, reached the market inscribed “GE POL” on the girdle.

For a colourless diamond, finding type II raises an alert: it could be HPHT-treated or lab-grown, and goes for advanced testing.
Determining the origin of colour
Disclosure

A treatment is fine. Hiding it is not.

On the report

The grade carries a mark, e.g. colour E (HPHT)*, with a comment: “This diamond has been processed by HPHT to modify its colour. Laserscribe on girdle: HPHT”.

At the counter

The seller must tell the buyer about any treatment, whether it is permanent, and how to care for the stone.

What we learned

Fracture filling · unstable

Flash effect, gas bubbles, cloudy areas, crackled or uneven drying. Damaged by heat, steam, ultrasonic, acid.

Laser drilling · stable

LDH: a tunnel from the surface. ILD/KM: feathers opened from the inclusion. Both disclosed.

Colour

Coating (unstable), HPHT and irradiation (stable). Type IIa browns can be whitened by HPHT.

Disclose

On the report and to the customer.
