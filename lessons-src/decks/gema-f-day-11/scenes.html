Section 01

Stability: resisting change

Stability is a gem's resistance to heat, light and chemicals. It decides how a gem must be worn, cleaned and repaired.

Heat and thermal shock

Heat and sudden change

  • Heat can change colour, burn organics and melt fillings: amber softens at around 200 °C.
  • Thermal shock: a sudden change of temperature cracks a stone; fluid inclusions expand and burst.
  • Quench crackling: heated stones plunged into cold liquid craze all over (deliberate in some imitations).
  • The jeweller's torch during repairs is a common cause of damage.
Light, and losing water or oil

Fading and drying

Light

Fading

Some colours fade in daylight: heat-treated zircon can revert; amethyst, rose quartz and kunzite can pale.

Drying

Crazing

Opal can craze (fine surface cracks) if it loses water; oiled emeralds lose their oil and fissures reappear.

Advice: store fading stones away from strong light; don't leave opal in hot, dry places such as shop windows under lamps.
Acids, cosmetics, perfume, sweat

Chemical attack

Calcium carbonate materials (pearl, coral, shell) react even with weak acids such as vinegar, lemon juice and perspiration. Porous materials (turquoise, lapis) absorb oils, soaps and dyes and can discolour.

Section 02

Cleaning without damage

The safest method is almost always the gentlest one.

Three methods

From safest to riskiest

Safe for almost all

Warm soapy water

Mild detergent, a soft brush, rinse, dry with a soft cloth. Not for porous or organic materials soaked for long.

Use with caution

Ultrasonic cleaner

Vibrations in a liquid. Can crack stones with fractures, inclusions or cleavage, and remove fillings.

Use with caution

Steam cleaner

High-pressure steam: heat plus thermal shock. Avoid for heat-sensitive and fractured stones.

For pearls, opal, coral, amber and turquoise: wipe with a soft damp cloth only.

At the bench

Setting, sizing and repair

  • Pressure from claws can chip brittle or cleavable stones (zircon, topaz, tanzanite).
  • Remove the stone before using a torch for sizing or re-tipping, unless it is known to be heat-safe.
  • Avoid pickling acids with pearls, coral, lead-glass filled rubies.
  • Protective settings (bezel) help soft or brittle stones in rings.
Role play · what would you tell them?

Advise the customer

"Can I wear my opal ring every day?"
Advice

With care

Remove it for manual work; avoid knocks, heat and drying; a protective setting helps.

"How do I clean my pearls?"
Advice

Last on, first off

Put them on after perfume and make-up; wipe with a soft cloth; restring regularly.

"My emerald looks cloudier"
Advice

Possible oil loss

Probably oil drying out; it can be re-oiled by a professional. Never ultrasonic.

"Can you resize my ruby ring?"
Advice

Check first

Is it lead-glass filled? If so, remove it before heat and avoid pickling.

"Why is my blue zircon greener?"
Advice

Light exposure

Some heat-treated zircon reverts in light; store it in the dark.

"My turquoise changed colour"
Advice

Absorbed oils

Porous: skin oils and cosmetics discolour it. Keep away from creams.

Pearls are among the least stable gem materials: soft, porous and attacked by weak acids. "Last on, first off" is the golden rule.

Did you know?
Cleopatra

A pearl in vinegar?

Legend says Cleopatra dissolved a pearl in vinegar to win a bet. Calcium carbonate does react with acid, though slowly.

Shop windows

Lights are hot

Display lighting can dry out opals and fade some colours: rotate stock out of the window.

Ultrasonic

Invented for industry

Ultrasonic cleaners use cavitation: tiny bubbles collapsing, which also stress stones.
