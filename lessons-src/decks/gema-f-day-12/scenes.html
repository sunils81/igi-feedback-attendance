Section 03

Materials and structures

From atoms to crystal systems, forms and twins.

Chapter 3 at a glance

Materials and structures

Chemistry

Classification

Elements (diamond), oxides (corundum, spinel), silicates (beryl, quartz, garnet), carbonates (pearl, malachite).

Structure

Order

Crystalline vs amorphous (glass, opal); single crystal vs polycrystalline (chalcedony, jade).

Systems

Seven

Cubic, tetragonal, trigonal, hexagonal, orthorhombic, monoclinic, triclinic.

Forms and habits

Shape

Octahedra, prisms, barrels, plates; terminations; massive.

Surface

Markings

Striations (tourmaline lengthwise, quartz across), trigons, etch pits.

Twins

Four types

Macle, lamellar, trilling, interpenetrant.

Theory · 6 marks

Explain the difference between a synthetic and an imitation, giving an example of each, and state why the difference matters to the trade.

Two marks each for the definitions with an example, two for the trade reason.

1Synthetic: man-made, with essentially the same composition and structure as a natural material,
1e.g. synthetic ruby (flame fusion).
1Imitation: imitates the appearance only, with different properties,
1e.g. red glass imitating ruby.
1Both must be disclosed: value is very different from the natural gem.
1Synthetics need gemmological tests to detect; imitations usually show different properties (RI, SG, inclusions).
