Section 01

The loupe, properly

Steady hands, the right distance, the right light.

Loupe drill · check yourself

Seven-point checklist

  • Clean the stone; hold it in tweezers by the girdle.
  • Loupe close to the eye; keep both eyes open.
  • Brace your hands together, elbows on the bench.
  • Move the stone to focus (about 2.5 cm away), not the loupe.
  • Light from the side for inclusions; reflected light for the surface.
  • Scan the table, then the crown, then through the pavilion.
  • Rotate the stone in the tweezers; look from several directions.

Same order, every time

Order makes you thorough, and makes your report easy to mark.

Cut stone · unaided eye → loupe → microscope

The observation sequence

1 · Eye

Overview

Colour, lustre, transparency, shape and cut, effects. Measure.

2 · Loupe

Surface

Facet edges, polish, scratches, joins, coatings.

3 · Loupe

Inside

Inclusions, zoning, doubling.

4 · Microscope

Detail

Darkfield, brightfield, top light; confirm and sketch.

5 · Conclude

Evidence

"Consistent with…" unless the evidence is conclusive.

Reports that earn marks

Precise words, a clear sketch, a supported conclusion.

Spot the difference

Weak line → strong line

LineWeakStrong
Colour"Nice red""Medium-dark, strong, slightly purplish red; even"
Lustre"Shiny""Bright vitreous"
Cut"Oval""Oval mixed cut: brilliant crown, step pavilion"
External"Some marks""Fine polish lines on the table; abraded facet edges"

How a report is marked

What the marker looks for

  • Every line present, in order.
  • Precise vocabulary from the word bank.
  • Features described by shape, number, size, colour and position.
  • A sketch: large, features placed, all labelled.
  • A conclusion the observations support, and no more.
