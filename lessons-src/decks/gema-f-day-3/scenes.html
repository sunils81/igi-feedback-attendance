Before you look

Clean stone. Right light.

Handheld light

Incandescent white light. Use it as reflected light (surfaces) or transmitted light (through the stone).

LED flat light

Diffused white light, transmitted only. Best for colour zoning and transparency.

Gem cloth

Dust, grease and fingerprints change the surface and can be mistaken for inclusions.

Then, unaided eye first. You can discern a huge amount before you pick up the loupe: colour, lustre, transparency, shape and cut, measurements and optical effects.

Colour

Assessed against a white background, in white light, and described in three parts.

Describing colour

Hue · tone · saturation

H

Hue

Your first impression: green, blue, yellow. A secondary colour comes first as "-ish": yellowish green.

T

Tone

How light or dark: light or dark yellowish green.

S

Saturation

Intensity: vivid, intense at the top; dull, faint at the bottom.

High saturation + dark tone = deepLow saturation + light tone = paleLow saturation + dark tone = greyish / brownish

Name the colour like a gemmologist

Vivid yellowish green: a strong, pure colour with a yellow secondary hue (demantoid garnet).
Deep violetish blue: dark tone with high saturation, a slight violet secondary hue (sapphire).
Intense yellow: high saturation, medium tone. No obvious secondary hue.

Transparency (diaphaneity)

Hold it up to a strong light: how much passes through?

Transparent

Objects behind are clearly seen.

Translucent

Light passes, but images are blurred or lost.

Opaque

No light passes through.

Some "opaque" stones let a little light through from a strong source: these can be described as semi-translucent. Report what you actually see.

Section 03

Shape and style of cut

Shape is the outline. Style of cut is how it is fashioned. Report both.

Two separate words on your report
Shape · the outline

Round · Oval · Square · Rectangular · Hexagonal · Octagonal · Triangular · Pear · Heart · Marquise (navette) · Cushion

Style of cut · the form

Faceted: brilliant · step · mixed · rose · fantasy
Non-faceted: cabochon · bead · carving · cameo / intaglio · freeform

Report example: "pear-shaped mixed cut" or "oval cabochon": shape first, then style.
The most popular cut

Modern round brilliant

57

facets, or 58 if there is a culet facet.

  • The best balance of brilliance (light return) and fire (dispersion).
  • Most used for diamond.
  • The girdle is the "waist" of the gem, between crown and pavilion.
Faceted styles

Brilliant, step, mixed, and the rest

Brilliant shapes

Oval, pear, marquise, heart, princess: brilliant faceting in other outlines.

Step cut

Long, narrow facets parallel to the girdle. Needs good clarity. Emerald cut: truncated corners.

Mixed cut

Two styles: usually a brilliant crown and a step pavilion. Common for coloured gems.

Rose and briolette

Older styles: a domed crown of triangular facets with a flat base; or a drop faceted all round.

Non-faceted styles

Cabochons: why choose a dome?

  • To protect materials with poorer durability.
  • To use opaque or very dark material.
  • To use lower-quality, heavily included material.
  • To show an optical effect: star, cat's eye, play of colour.

Carvings, cameos and intaglios

Measurements

Length × width × depth, in mm

  • For the Foundation, record approximate dimensions with a millimetre ruler.
  • Round stones: two diameters and the depth.
  • A dial or digital gauge measures to 2 decimal places: Chapter 5, where measurements help estimate weight.
Example: "pear-shaped mixed cut, approx. 7 × 5 × 3.5 mm".

Light meets structure

Asterism
Star ruby / sapphire

Asterism

A star of light from aligned needle-like inclusions (silk).

Chatoyancy
Cat's-eye chrysoberyl

Chatoyancy

A single band of light, like a cat's eye, from parallel fibres or needles.

Fire
Diamond · demantoid

Fire (dispersion)

White light split into spectral colours. Chapter 7.

Aventurescence
Aventurine · sunstone · goldstone

Aventurescence

Glittering reflections from small platy inclusions.

Iridescence
Thin films and layers

Iridescence

Rainbow colours from interference of light in thin layers or cracks.

Play of colour
Precious opal

Play of colour

Patches of spectral colour that move as the stone turns, from its silica-sphere structure.

Labradorescence
Feldspar · labradorite

Labradorescence

Flashes of blue, green or gold from thin internal layers.

Adularescence
Feldspar · moonstone

Adularescence

A soft, floating bluish-white sheen.
