Bench stations · rotate on your mentor's signal

Six stations, one skill each

Station 1 · colour

Describe hue, tone and saturation for 5 stones on white. Find one with colour zoning on the flat light.

Station 2 · lustre & transparency

Rank 5 stones by lustre. Classify transparent, translucent, opaque.

Station 3 · shape, cut, size

Name shape and style of cut for 6 stones; measure L × W × D with a ruler.

Station 4 · surface

Reflected light and loupe: polish lines, facet edge condition, scratches, any junction or coating.

Station 5 · inclusions

Loupe then darkfield microscope: describe shape, number, size, phases. Sketch them.

Station 6 · optical effects

Black background, pinpoint light: star, cat's eye, play of colour, adularescence.

What examiners see too often

Six mistakes to avoid

  • Observing a dirty stone: dust described as inclusions.
  • Colour judged on a coloured surface or under yellow light.
  • "Brilliant" written as a shape: shape and style of cut are two separate words.
  • Vague words: "some inclusions". Say what shape, how many, how big, what colour.
  • No diagram, or a diagram without labels and position.
  • A conclusion that the evidence does not support. "Consistent with…" is better than a guess.
Gem of the Day · 5 of 60

Corundum

CompositionAluminium oxide, Al₂O₃Crystal systemTrigonal; barrel-shaped and tabular crystalsHardness9SG≈ 4.00RI1.762 – 1.770 · DR 0.008 – 0.010VarietiesRuby (Cr) · blue sapphire (Fe + Ti) · fancy sapphires · padparadschaTreatmentsHeating (very common), lead-glass filling, diffusion

Corundum was the first gem to be synthesised commercially, by Verneuil in 1902. That is why curved striae and gas bubbles are among the first things a gemmologist looks for in a red or blue stone.

Theory · 4 marks

Describe two features seen with magnification that would separate a natural ruby from a flame-fusion synthetic ruby.

Two features for each: what you would see.

1Natural: straight, angular colour zoning following the crystal faces.
1Natural: mineral inclusions such as rutile needles (silk), crystals, fingerprints.
1Synthetic: curved striae or colour bands, best seen with diffused light.
1Synthetic: gas bubbles, round or elongated.
