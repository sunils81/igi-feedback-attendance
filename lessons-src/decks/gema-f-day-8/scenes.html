Faces, forms and habits

A crystal's faces are not random: they follow its symmetry. The overall shape it favours is its habit.

Crystal form

A form is a set of matching faces

A form is a group of faces related by the crystal's symmetry: produce one face and symmetry produces the rest.

  • Closed forms enclose space on their own: cube (6 faces), octahedron (8), rhombic dodecahedron (12).
  • Open forms cannot: a prism is open at both ends and needs other faces to close it.
  • A pinacoid is a pair of parallel faces, such as the flat top of a beryl prism.
Most crystals combine several forms

Prism + pyramid + pinacoid

Prism

The sides

Faces parallel to the c-axis: the long sides of the crystal.

Pyramid / bipyramid

The point

Faces that meet the c-axis at an angle: one end, or both.

Pinacoid

The flat end

A pair of parallel faces cutting across the c-axis.

A zircon crystal is typically a square prism capped by pyramids at each end; a beryl crystal a hexagonal prism closed by a flat pinacoid.
Crystal habit · the characteristic overall shape

Same species, typical shape

Habit depends on growth conditions as well as structure, so it is a clue, not proof. Massive and botryoidal habits describe aggregates such as chalcedony and malachite.

How does the crystal end?

Terminations

A topaz crystal often ends in a smooth flat surface that is not a crystal face: it is a cleavage plane where the crystal broke from the rock.

Reading the surface

Crystal faces carry a record of growth and dissolution. These marks help you identify rough, and sometimes polished stones.

Striations · parallel grooves on crystal faces

Direction matters

GemStriations
TourmalineLengthwise, parallel to the c-axis
QuartzAcross the prism faces (horizontal)
TopazLengthwise on the prism faces
CorundumHorizontal on bipyramid faces; triangular on basal faces
Remember: tourmaline lengthwise, quartz across. It separates two prismatic crystals at a glance.
Growth marks and etch features

Marks of growth and dissolution

Trigons

Diamond

Small triangular pits on octahedral faces, pointing the opposite way to the face.

Etch pits

Beryl, topaz

Formed when fluids partly dissolve a crystal after growth.

Growth hillocks

Many gems

Low mounds and steps recording layers of growth.

Do markings survive cutting?

Clues left on cut stones

Diamond

Naturals

A small part of the original crystal surface left on the girdle, sometimes with trigons: proof of a natural crystal face.

Any gem

Growth zoning

Internal growth lines that follow the crystal's faces: straight and angular in natural stones.

Composites and coatings

Not markings

A junction line or iridescent film is not a growth feature: check the surface in reflected light.

When crystals grow as one

A twin is two or more parts of one crystal that grew together in a fixed, symmetrical relationship.

Twinned crystals

One crystal, reoriented parts

  • The parts share a common plane or axis: the twin plane or twin axis.
  • The atomic structure is continuous across the boundary, but changes orientation.
  • Twins often show re-entrant angles: notches that point inwards.
  • Twinning can show as lines or planes inside a cut stone.
Parallel growth is not twinning: several crystals growing side by side in the same orientation.
Types of twinning

Contact, lamellar, cyclic, interpenetrant

Contact twin
Diamond, spinel

Macle

Two halves joined on one plane: flat triangular diamond macles; spinel-law twins.

Repeated twin
Corundum, labradorite

Lamellar

Many thin parallel twin layers: lines in ruby, the sheen of labradorite.

Cyclic twin
Chrysoberyl

Trilling

Three crystals around a centre: pseudo-hexagonal alexandrite.

Penetration twin
Fluorite

Interpenetrant

Two crystals grow through each other: fluorite cubes.

Diagrams to sketch in your notebook

Twin shapes

In a cut gem, lamellar twinning shows as sets of fine straight lines that may cross at angles; in corundum these planes can also be planes of weakness (parting).

Named after places

Twin laws carry names such as "spinel law", "Japan law" (quartz) and "Brazil law".
