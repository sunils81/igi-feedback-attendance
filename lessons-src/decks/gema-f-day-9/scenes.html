Section 01

A method for rough

Look in a fixed order and write as you look: the description builds itself.

Describing a crystal · always in this order

Five steps

1

Overall shape

Habit: prismatic, tabular, octahedral, barrel-shaped, massive…

2

Cross-section

Look down the length: square, triangle, hexagon, rhomb?

3

Terminations

Pointed, flat, double, cleaved or broken?

4

Surface

Striations (which direction?), trigons, etch pits, lustre.

5

Twins and colour

Re-entrant angles, twin planes; colour and any zoning or pleochroism.

Then put it together: "a hexagonal prism with a flat termination and fine lengthwise striations: consistent with beryl".
Be careful

Shape is a clue, not proof

  • Water-worn pebbles have lost their faces (sapphire and spinel from gem gravels).
  • Broken fragments and cleavage pieces show no crystal form.
  • Synthetic crystals can grow with natural-looking faces.
  • Several gems share a system: always combine shape with other evidence.
Write "consistent with…" when the evidence is only the crystal shape.

A gallery of gem crystals

The gems you will meet again and again, grouped by crystal system.

Cubic

Diamond, spinel, garnet, fluorite

Diamond
Look for

Octahedra; trigons

Often rounded or distorted octahedra; flat triangular macles. Adamantine lustre.

Spinel
Look for

Sharp octahedra

Bright octahedra; spinel-law twins with re-entrant angles.

Garnet
Look for

Dodecahedra

Rhombic dodecahedra or icositetrahedra; often rounded.

Fluorite
Look for

Cubes, octahedra

Cubes and interpenetrant twins; octahedra from cleavage.

Cubic crystals look "equal in all directions": no long axis.

Trigonal and hexagonal

Corundum, quartz, tourmaline, beryl

Corundum
Look for

Barrels and plates

Sapphire: barrel-shaped bipyramids. Ruby: flat tabular plates. Triangular marks on basal faces.

Quartz
Look for

Pointed hexagonal prism

Six-sided prism, horizontal striations, pointed (rhombohedral) termination.

Tourmaline
Look for

Rounded triangle

Long prism, lengthwise striations, rounded triangular section.

Beryl
Look for

Flat-topped hexagon

Hexagonal prism, flat pinacoid termination; etch pits.

Quartz and beryl both show six-sided prisms: check the termination and the striations.

Tetragonal, orthorhombic and lower

Zircon, topaz, peridot, chrysoberyl

Zircon
Look for

Square prism + pyramids

Tetragonal; adamantine-like lustre; often brownish.

Topaz
Look for

Prism + cleaved end

Orthorhombic; lengthwise striations; flat basal cleavage.

Peridot
Look for

Flattened prisms

Orthorhombic; usually found as broken or water-worn pieces.

Chrysoberyl
Look for

Trillings

Orthorhombic; pseudo-hexagonal cyclic twins; tabular.

Monoclinic and triclinic gems (orthoclase, spodumene, kyanite) show inclined faces and no right-angled "box" shape.

Section 03

Draw what you see

A sketch makes you look harder, and it is the clearest way to show an examiner what you observed.

Sketching a crystal

Six rules

  • Draw large: at least half a page width.
  • Start with the c-axis as a faint vertical line.
  • Draw the prism outline, then the terminations.
  • Use a ruler for straight edges; show hidden edges dashed if needed.
  • Add striations in their true direction and any twin lines.
  • Label: habit, system, markings, colour; name and date.
