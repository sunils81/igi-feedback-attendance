The three words every customer now asks about

Real, real-but-grown, or just pretending?

Natural

Formed by nature

Grown deep in the earth over millions, even billions, of years.

Lab-grown (synthetic)

Real, but grown

Same chemistry and structure as natural, made in weeks in a factory. Must always be disclosed.

Imitation (simulant)

Just pretending

Looks like the real thing but is a different material: glass, cubic zirconia and others.

By the end of DG you will separate all three, and you will know what must be disclosed on every sale.
Story · the largest gem-quality rough diamond ever found

3,106 carats

  • Found in 1905 at the Premier Mine, South Africa: about the size of a fist.
  • Cut in 1908 by the Asscher company in Amsterdam.
  • It became 9 major stones and about 96 smaller ones.
  • The largest, Cullinan I (530.2 ct), sits in the British Sovereign's Sceptre; Cullinan II in the Imperial State Crown.
Every one of those decisions (where to cleave, how to cut, what it would be worth) is a skill you start learning in this course.

Puzzle 2 · what is happening here?

Green by day, red by night

  • Not two stones: one alexandrite, a variety of chrysoberyl.
  • Chromium absorbs light so that daylight brings out green and warm lamplight brings out red.
  • Found in Russia's Ural Mountains in the 1830s; green and red were imperial Russia's colours.
Colour change, play of colour, cat's eyes, stars: the optical effects you will learn to explain and identify.

Why the trade needs you

Selling with integrity

  • Natural, lab-grown, treated, imitation: each must be named honestly on every sale.
  • A wrong description costs money, reputation, and sometimes a court case.
  • Customers now ask "is it lab-grown?" before they ask the price.
  • The answer must come from evidence: observation, instruments, and a report.
That is what an IGI-trained professional brings to any business: the confidence to say exactly what a stone is.

Lab rules · stones and instruments are valuable

How we work in the lab

  • Use tweezers, by the girdle; work low over the tray.
  • Only one stone out of its paper at a time; never mix parcels.
  • Dropped a stone? Stop, tell the instructor, and don't move your chair.
  • Handle instruments gently; switch lamps off after use.
  • Be on time; phones away during class and tests.

Why coloured stones hold their appeal

Rare beats replicable

  • Each natural ruby, sapphire or emerald is unique: colour, inclusions, origin.
  • Most are treated: someone has to detect it and disclose it.
  • Synthetics and imitations exist for every major gem: someone has to separate them.
  • Birthstones, Navratna, colour in bridal and fashion jewellery: Indian demand is deep-rooted.
A DG who adds CSG can identify any gem a customer brings in, not just diamonds.
