Just carbon

The hardest natural material on Earth is made of the same element as the "lead" in your pencil. The difference is how the atoms hold hands.

Same element, two personalities · tap to compare

Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon

The Mohs scale of hardness · tap a mineral

Hardest? Yes. Unbreakable? No.

Mohs 10

Diamond

Hardest of all. Only a diamond can scratch a diamond.

Hardness ≠ toughness. Hardness is resistance to scratching. A sharp knock on a thin edge or a point can still chip or split a diamond along its cleavage. Say "the hardest natural material", never "unbreakable".

Three things most people never hear about diamond

🏛️
Where does the word "diamond" come from?
Tap to reveal

Greek: adamas

It means "unconquerable" or "invincible". The ancient Greeks believed nothing could defeat it.

🔥
Can a diamond burn?
Tap to reveal

Yes, it can

It is carbon, so at very high temperatures in air it burns away, like coal. That is why bench jewellers are careful with the torch when repairing a ring.

🌡️
Why does a diamond feel cold on your lip?
Tap to reveal

It moves heat brilliantly

Diamond carries heat away better than any other gem. Simple "diamond testers" use exactly this: they measure how fast heat flows through the stone.

Where natural diamonds come from

India was the world's only source for 2,000 years

📜 India, the first home of diamonds

For about two thousand years, every diamond known came from India, from the river gravels around Golconda (today's Telangana and Andhra Pradesh). The Koh-i-Noor and the stone that became the Hope Diamond are Golconda diamonds.

🇧🇷 1720s · Brazil

Diamonds found in Brazil ended India's monopoly.

🇿🇦 1867 · South Africa

A diamond picked up near the Orange River started the modern rush. The town of Kimberley gave its name to kimberlite, the rock that carries diamonds.

Major producers today

Russia, Botswana, Canada, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Australia, Angola, South Africa and Namibia.

India today

India mines very little: its active diamond mine is near Panna, Madhya Pradesh. But India cuts and polishes about 90% of the world's diamonds, mostly in Surat. Rough comes in, polished goes out.

That is why IGI has a big presence in India: the diamonds of the world pass through here.

Two ways to grow a diamond · press the buttons

HPHT recreates the mantle. CVD builds it atom layer by atom layer.

HPHT · High Pressure, High Temperature

A carbon source, a metal solvent and a tiny diamond seed are squeezed in a giant press at mantle-like pressure and heat. The first reproducible diamond growth was HPHT, in 1954.

CVD · Chemical Vapour Deposition

In a vacuum chamber, a gas rich in carbon is turned into plasma. Carbon atoms rain down onto thin diamond seed plates and the crystal grows upwards, layer by layer, over a few weeks.

Game · sort the cards

Natural, laboratory-grown, or not a diamond at all?

Especially for marketing · India's diamond terminology standard, IS 19469:2025 (Bureau of Indian Standards, January 2026)

Words we use, and words we avoid

Under the standard, "diamond" on its own means a natural diamond, and laboratory-grown stones are always disclosed with the full term. Short forms like "LGD" or "lab-grown" are fine in internal chats, never in anything a customer reads. When in doubt, check with the HOD before publishing.

1954

Scientists at General Electric in the USA grew diamonds with an HPHT press in 1954. Gem-quality crystals came much later.

Tap to reveal

India, mostly Surat

Surat cuts most of the world’s polished diamonds, natural and laboratory-grown, and India is also a leading grower.

Tap to reveal

Not reliably

They look identical. Telling them apart needs advanced instruments, which is exactly what IGI’s screening and reports provide.

From rough to radiant

A rough diamond looks like a cloudy pebble. Planning, sawing, bruting and polishing turn it into the sparkling stone in the window, and most of its weight is lost on the way.

Rough crystal shapes · drag to spin, tap a shape

What a diamond looks like before it is cut

Octahedrondrag to rotate

Octahedron · two pyramids base to base. The classic diamond crystal and the cutter's favourite: one octahedron can give two round brilliants.

A CVD crystal grows as a flat, square-ish tablet on its seed plate: no octahedron at all. Shape is one clue a cutter uses.

From the mine to the wheel

⛏️
How much rock is moved for one polished carat?
Tap to reveal

Often hundreds of tonnes

In many mines it takes on the order of 200–250 tonnes of ore to find enough rough for a single one-carat polished diamond.

📐
Who worked out the "ideal" round brilliant?
Tap to reveal

Marcel Tolkowsky, 1919

A young engineer from a diamond-cutting family calculated the angles that return the most light. Modern round brilliants are still close to his design.

🏙️
How many diamonds pass through Surat?
Tap to reveal

About 9 out of 10

India cuts and polishes about 90% of the world’s diamonds, most of them in Surat, home to one of the world’s largest office buildings, the Surat Diamond Bourse.

Carbon, locked tight

Born deep, very old

150–200 km down, 1–3.5 billion years ago; brought up in kimberlite. India was the first source; Surat cuts most of the world's diamonds today.

Laboratory-grown
