Evolution of diamonds

From carbon, deep in the Earth, to the stone in your showcase.From carbon to crystal: how diamonds form in the Earth, how they are grown above it, and how both become the stone in your showcase.From carbon to crystal: formed in the Earth or grown above it, then cut and polished for your showcase.

What is diamond made of?

One element: carbon

  • Diamond is pure carbon, the same element as coal and pencil graphite.
  • The difference is the arrangement. In diamond, every carbon atom locks onto four neighbours in a rigid 3D lattice.
  • That lattice makes diamond the hardest natural material (10 on the Mohs scale) and gives it a refractive index of 2.42: the reason it sparkles.
  • Carbon has 6 electrons: 2 in the inner shell and 4 in the outer shell. Those 4 are the bonds.
Coal and diamond: same atoms, different arrangement, completely different material.

Natural diamonds · where do we find them?

Born 250–300 km down

Depth250–300 km
Heat1500–1800 °C
Pressure45–60 kbar
  • Natural diamonds form deep in the Earth, where the heat and pressure lock carbon into the diamond lattice.
  • Most are billions of years old, far older than almost anything a customer will ever own.
  • Deep volcanic eruptions carried them to the surface in rock called kimberlite and lamproite.
The volcanic express · tap a rock

Two rocks bring diamonds up

Kimberlite

Kimberlite

The main source rock of natural diamonds, named after Kimberley in South Africa. It rose fast from deep in the mantle in narrow "pipes".

Lamproite

Lamproite

A second, rarer volcanic rock that carries diamonds, best known from Western Australia (the Argyle mine).

The mine

Open-pit mining

Miners follow the pipe down. Tonnes of rock are crushed and processed to recover a few carats of rough diamond.

Rough diamond crystals · turn the crystal
Rough crystal shapesDrag to turn

Before the sparkle: rough

The most common natural crystal is the octahedron: two pyramids base to base. Cutters plan the polished diamond around the crystal's shape.
Assorting diamond crystals

Thousands of categories

  • Rough is sorted by size, shape, colour and quality before it is sold or cut.
  • Each category has a different value and a different cutting plan.
  • This is why two diamonds of the same weight can be worlds apart in price.
Laboratory-grown diamonds · two methods

Same carbon, grown in weeks above ground

CVD · Chemical Vapour Deposition

Built layer by layer

A thin diamond seed plate sits in a vacuum chamber. Methane and hydrogen are energised into a plasma, and carbon settles onto the seed, layer by layer, over several weeks. Crystals grow as flat, square-ish blocks.

HPHT · High Pressure, High Temperature

The Earth's recipe, in a press

A large press recreates mantle-like heat and pressure. Carbon dissolves in molten metal and crystallises on a diamond seed. Crystals grow as cube-octahedra (cuboctahedra).

Both methods grow real diamond: the same carbon lattice, hardness and sparkle. Only the origin differs, and it must always be disclosed.
Diamond rough crystals · natural, CVD and HPHT

You can't tell once they are polished

The rough shapes differ. After cutting and polishing, only a gem lab with advanced instruments can tell the origin. That is why the IGI report matters.
From rough to polished · click to follow the stone

Rough to polish in four steps

STEP 1

Planning

The rough is scanned and mapped to get the most beauty and weight from it.

STEP 2

Sawing

A laser or blade splits the crystal along the plan.

STEP 3

Bruting

Two diamonds grind against each other to give the round outline (the girdle).

STEP 4

Polishing

Facets are ground one by one on a spinning wheel coated with diamond powder. A round brilliant has 57 or 58.

Only diamond cuts diamond. Natural and lab-grown rough go through the same cutting process.India cuts and polishes most of the world's diamonds.

Look-alikes are not diamonds

DiamondNatural or lab-grown

Diamond

Carbon. Hardness 10. Refractive index 2.42. Strong brilliance and fire, crisp facet edges.

Cubic zirconiaCZ

Cubic zirconia

Zirconium oxide, made in a lab. Softer (about 8–8.5), heavier, lower refractive index. Facet edges wear and look rounded.

MoissaniteSilicon carbide

Moissanite

Very rare in nature; the gem material is lab-made. Hardness about 9.25, higher refractive index and very strong fire. Doubly refractive: facet edges look doubled.

A basic thermal "diamond tester" can be fooled by moissanite, and it cannot tell natural from lab-grown. The IGI report is the proof.

Cut is craftsmanship, not shape

Round, oval or pear is the shape. Cut is the quality of the angles, proportions and polish that decide how much light dances inside the stone.

Scintillation

The sparkle: the pattern of light and dark flashes when the diamond or the light moves.

Hard customer question

"The stone in the other shop looked bigger. Why does yours look smaller?"

That's cut. A shallow stone can look bigger face-up but leaks light out of the bottom and looks dull in normal light. A well-cut stone returns more light to the eye.

"Which would you rather wear: a stone that looks big in the shop, or one that catches every eye at the dinner table?"

C4 · Colour · slide from D to Z

Platinum or white gold: suggest D–H. Yellow gold flatters I–K. Beyond Z, a diamond becomes a fancy colour.
How colour is graded · degrees of colour and the master set

Graded face-down, against master stones

Colour is judged face-down on white, under controlled light. In the store, show stones on a white card so the customer sees honest colour.
Fancy colours · beyond Z

Yellow, orange, green, pink, blue…

Fancy colours are graded differently: by hue and intensity, not D–Z. Colour can be natural or created by treatment, or grown that way in a lab. The report says which.
Fluorescence · switch the UV lamp on

A glow under UV light

  • Graded None, Faint, Medium, Strong, Very Strong on the IGI report. Most often blue.
  • Usually invisible in normal light. Very strong fluorescence can occasionally make a stone look slightly hazy.
  • Under UV, stones in one piece can glow differently: a useful clue when screening jewellery.

Treatments, screening & inscription

What can be done to a diamond, how labs and stores catch it, and the tiny number that proves which diamond is which.

Identification of treated diamonds · tap each treatment

Treatments must always be disclosed

Fracture filling

Fracture filling

A glass-like filler hides cracks to improve apparent clarity. Shows a "flash effect". Not durable to heat.

Laser drilling

Laser drilling

A tiny laser channel reaches a dark inclusion so it can be bleached. The channel stays visible under magnification.

HPHT colour treatment

HPHT treatment

Heat and pressure change the colour, for example brownish to whiter, or create fancy colours. Needs lab detection.

Irradiation & coating

Irradiation & coating

Radiation, often followed by heating, or a surface coating, creates or changes colour. Coatings can wear off.

If a diamond is treated, the IGI report says so. Tell the customer before the sale, on the tag and on the invoice. Lab-grown diamonds may also have a post-growth treatment; the report states it.

Small stones, big risk

  • Screening checks loose parcels and set jewellery and flags stones that need further testing.

Laser inscription · transparency you can see

The report number, on the diamond

01 · Permanent identification

The IGI report number is laser-inscribed on the girdle, the thin edge of the diamond. On a lab-grown diamond the inscription also says it is laboratory-grown.

02 · Consumer protection

The diamond's identity is recorded permanently: for the buyer and for the store.

03 · Confidence

Invisible to the eye, readable with a 10× loupe. The customer can match it to the report and verify it online.

The objections a natural-diamond store hears most

Respond with facts, never by running anything down

"I read lab-grown is the same and much cheaper. Why natural?"

"Both are real diamond. Natural is rare and finite, and over a billion years old. If that story matters to you, that's what you're choosing, and the IGI report proves it."

"How do I know it's really natural?"

"Every centre stone has an IGI natural diamond report and a girdle inscription. Let's match it and verify it online together."

"Are the small stones natural too?"

"Every parcel we buy is screened, and this piece has an IGI jewellery report that states the origin of the diamonds. Let me show you."
Never call lab-grown "fake" to win the sale, never guess origin by eye, and never promise prices will rise.
The objections a lab-grown store hears most

Honest, confident, with the report in hand

"Is this a fake diamond?"

"Not at all. It's diamond, the same carbon crystal as natural, only grown above ground. Your IGI report confirms it."

"Why is it so much cheaper? What's the catch?"

"No catch. The difference is origin: weeks in a reactor rather than billions of years and a mine. Same grading, same sparkle."

"My wife will think I went cheap."

"What matters most to her: the design, the size, the sparkle? Many couples choose lab-grown to get exactly the ring they want."
Never say "nobody will know", never call it "natural", and never promise investment or resale value.
Natural and lab-grown · handle with balance

Never sell one origin by putting down the other

"Is lab-grown fake?"

"No. It's real diamond, grown above ground. Natural formed in the Earth. Both are graded the same way, and the IGI report tells you which is which."

"Which should I buy?"

"If rarity and a billion-year story matter, natural. If you want more size or grade for the budget, lab-grown. Let me show you one of each, side by side."

"My family only wants natural."

"That's a beautiful tradition. Let me show you our natural collection, each with its IGI report, so the story is documented."
Never say "real vs fake", never mix stock without labels, and never promise investment value for either origin.

Disclosure · the non-negotiables

Trust takes years to build and one sentence to lose

✓ Always

  • Call a diamond natural only when it's verified: IGI report, inscription, screened parcel.
  • Say "lab-grown" (or laboratory-grown) every time you say "diamond", and before the sale.
  • Disclose any treatment before the sale, exactly as the report states it.
  • Put origin and treatment on the tag, the invoice and the report.
  • Keep natural and lab-grown stock separate and labelled.
  • Offer your exchange or buy-back policy in writing.

✕ Never

  • "Real" vs "fake". Both natural and lab-grown are real diamonds.
  • Call a lab-grown diamond "natural", or use "synthetic" to belittle it.
  • Guess origin by eye or with a basic tester, or sell unscreened melee as "all natural".
  • Promise investment or resale value, for either origin.
  • Create false urgency: "Prices go up next week."
